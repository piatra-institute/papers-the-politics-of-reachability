## The conceptual predecessors (T1)

- **Berlin (1969)**, *Four Essays on Liberty* (OUP): negative liberty as non-interference; the baseline the opening case defeats, since the missing valley interferes with no one.
- **Sen (1999)**, *Development as Freedom* (Knopf): capabilities as substantive freedom; the nearest predecessor. Reachability adds temporal ordering, path dependence, organizational access, and second-order power over the opportunity structure.
- **Pettit (1999 [1997])**, *Republicanism* (OUP, 10.1093/0198296428.001.0001): freedom as non-domination; a pathway passable only at a gatekeeper's discretion is domination in his sense even if permission is always granted.
- **Gibson (1979)**, *The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception* (Houghton Mifflin/Erlbaum): affordances; the environment-relative ancestor of reachability.
- **Rawls (1993)**, *Political Liberalism* (Columbia UP): the fair value of the political liberties.

## Oligarchy and landscape power (T1)

- **Winters and Page (2009)**, Perspectives on Politics 7(4): 731–751 (10.1017/s1537592709991770): oligarchy measured as material power concentration, compatible with formal democracy. **Winters (2011)**, *Oligarchy* (CUP, 10.1017/cbo9780511793806).
- **Robeyns (2019)**, Journal of Human Development and Capabilities 20(3): 251–266 (10.1080/19452829.2019.1633734): limitarianism; the democratic argument against extreme wealth, redirected here from holdings to landscape power.
- **Anderson (2017)**, *Private Government* (Princeton UP): arbitrary authority tolerated when called property.
- **Strange (1988)**, *States and Markets* (Pinter): structural power over frameworks; landscape power presented as its dynamic, topological specification.
- **Pistor (2019)**, *The Code of Capital* (Princeton UP): law as the technology that makes capital durable; law as landscape construction.
- **Deleuze (1992)**, "Postscript on the Societies of Control," October 59: 3–7: control as modulation; the register in which landscape operations read.

## Landscapes, feedback, plurality (T1)

- **Waddington (1942)**, Nature 150(3811): 563–565 (10.1038/150563a0): canalization. **Waddington (1957)**, *The Strategy of the Genes* (Allen & Unwin): the epigenetic landscape. Used as structure with the disanalogy stated: societies are reflexive and self-representing.
- **Arthur (1989)**, The Economic Journal 99(394): 116–131 (10.2307/2234208): increasing returns and lock-in; the feedback that makes canalization endogenous.
- **Ostrom (1990)**, *Governing the Commons* (CUP, 10.1017/cbo9780511807763); **Ostrom (2010)**, American Economic Review 100(3): 641–672 (10.1257/aer.100.3.641): polycentric governance beyond markets and states.
- **Unger (1987)**, *False Necessity* (CUP): anti-necessitarian institutional experimentalism. **Sabel and Zeitlin (2008)**, European Law Journal 14(3): 271–327 (10.1111/j.1468-0386.2008.00415.x): experimentalist governance as recursive revision.
- **Polanyi (1944)**, *The Great Transformation* (Farrar & Rinehart/Beacon): markets as institutionally constituted.
- **Mazzucato (2013)**, *The Entrepreneurial State* (Anthem): public investment upstream of private innovation.
- **Hayek (1945)**, American Economic Review 35(4): 519–530: dispersed knowledge; turned toward plural discovery procedures.
- **Kauffman (2000)**, *Investigations* (OUP): the adjacent possible; the object monopoly destroys.
- **Zittrain (2006)**, Harvard Law Review 119: 1974–2040 (verified via Harvard DASH): generativity; systems that let unanticipated audiences build unanticipated things.

## Cut or bounded, and why

- **Thatcher-era material** (parliamentary histories in the seed): the paper does not run a history of neoliberalism and does not make one government the origin; the privatization record enters only as one sentence of context, uncited, or is dropped.
- **Varoufakis and Žižek** (seed discussion): political commentary, no distinct analytic job beyond Winters/Anderson/Strange; dropped.
- **Jessop** (strategic selectivity): overlaps Strange's job; dropped to keep the power section to one lineage.
- **Sovereign Tech Agency, ARIA, NGI Zero** (seed examples): real programmes, but citing funders' self-descriptions adds provenance risk for little argumentative gain; the design section derives principles from the model instead, and the DMA carries the existing-instrument point.
- **Hirschman, Stiegler, Simondon, Wiener/Beer, Friston** (seed's wider constellation): each would add a name without a distinct job; logged per the freeze discipline.
- **Shepherd's *The Shape of Agency*** (2021): the reason the seed's alternative title was rejected; noted, no need to cite.
