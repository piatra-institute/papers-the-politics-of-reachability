# Brief

Written before research begins.

## Question

An engineer who wants to build a new foundational network architecture, open, interoperable, resistant to enclosure, faces no legal prohibition and finds almost no institutional path: no funding form, no organizational home, no way to survive while building it. The path is permitted and absent. What theory of freedom notices the difference, and what politics follows from taking it seriously?

## Claim

Freedom is reachability: the set of consequential states an agent can actually attain from its position, through existing institutional pathways, within its budget of money, time, risk, and permission. Four moves carry the paper:

1. **Reachability against its predecessors.** Negative liberty asks whether anyone interferes; resource accounts ask what a person holds; capability theory, the nearest predecessor, asks what people can do and be. Reachability adds the dynamic, path-dependent layer: opportunity is produced by temporally ordered institutional transitions, many consequential states are reachable only through organizations, and the structure that determines cost, risk, and permission along the way is itself an object of power.
2. **Institutional landscapes and canalization.** Laws, finance, standards, infrastructure, and social insurance form a transition structure, a landscape in Waddington's sense, used as structure rather than metaphor and with the disanalogy stated: societies are not embryos, and the landscape's reflexivity is the political question. Canalization is real and double-edged: every enabling constraint canalizes, and the distinction that matters is generative versus enclosing, the canal that enlarges the downstream field of action versus the canal that terminates in a gatekeeper.
3. **Landscape power.** Beyond path agency (moving through pathways) and organizational agency (coordinating others) sits the capacity to alter which pathways exist for everyone else. Extreme wealth matters because it converts into this second-order agency: the constitutional question about a billionaire is how large a change in other people's reachable sets one person may produce unilaterally, and the anomaly is that consequential agency is surrounded by authorization machinery everywhere except where it is called property. The principle is bounded landscape power; the engineering analogy is least privilege, and the operator's virtue is irrelevant, since constitutions exist for exactly the cases where virtue cannot be assumed.
4. **Agential fertility.** The positive criterion: a landscape is fertile when consequential pathways are broadly accessible, institutionally plural, generative for others, forkable, and revisable. The opposite of domination is a landscape nobody can privately freeze and everyone has some effective capacity to reshape. The corporate canal is the empirical anchor, run as contrastive genealogy: the publicly funded, openly standardized Internet and Web against the venture pathway, claimed as institutional plurality rather than public-sector purity.

## Kind

**formal-model** wrapped around a political synthesis — ships a simulation. `has_simulation: true`, `claims_target: results.json`.

The simulation renders the landscape literally: a transition graph of institutional pathways with costs, risks, and permission gates; agents across budget deciles; generations in which success feeds back into the landscape. Results the model must produce or refute:

- **Permission and reachability come apart**: with formal permissions identical for all, reachable consequential states vary steeply by budget decile; the missing valley is a measured object.
- **Canalization is endogenous**: with increasing-returns feedback, a plural landscape concentrates; trajectory entropy falls and one pathway's share rises without any change in law; bounding landscape power stabilizes the plurality.
- **Landscape power harms at zero price**: an incumbent spending returns on ridge-raising and fork-closing shrinks others' reachable sets while every price in the model stays put; the antitrust point as a number.
- **Enabling constraints beat both alternatives**: a flat landscape (no substrate) reaches little; an open substrate and an enclosed substrate both canalize, and the open one leaves the larger and more diverse downstream field, with the gap widening after a crisis that forkable landscapes survive and frozen ones do not.
- **The floor changes what can be attempted**: with ruin risk uninsured, long experimental paths are entered only from the top deciles; a floor moves experimentation down the distribution. Precarity collapses the reachable state space, measured.
- **Where the thesis fails**: regimes where the open substrate loses (coordination-heavy worlds with weak maintenance funding, where the enclosed canal's gatekeeper at least pays the maintainers), kept and reported.

Calibration is structural, not empirical; the numbers are facts about the model's geometry and the paper says so.

## Constraint

The paper stands alone, cites no PIATRA paper, and does not reuse the seed's prose (negate-pivot-dense throughout). It is not a history of neoliberalism, does not make Thatcher the single origin, does not claim the early Internet was stateless or corporate-free, and does not deliver fifty policies; the design section derives principles from the model's operations. The billionaire argument enters through landscape power rather than through a wealth cap; the paper proposes no numeric cap.

## Cornerstone literature

Must engage, each with one job and one stated limit:

- **Waddington** — the landscape and canalization, with the disanalogy stated plainly.
- **Sen** — capabilities, the nearest predecessor; reachability as its dynamic, institutional extension.
- **Berlin** — negative liberty, the baseline the opening case defeats.
- **Pettit** — non-domination; reachability under a gatekeeper's discretionary permission is domination in his sense.
- **Winters and Page**; **Winters** — oligarchy as material power, compatible with elections.
- **Robeyns** — limitarianism; the democratic argument against extreme wealth, which the paper redirects from holdings to landscape power.
- **Anderson** — private government; arbitrary authority tolerated when described as property.
- **Rawls** — the fair value of political liberties.
- **Ostrom** — polycentric governance; commons as first-class institutional form, neither state nor market.
- **Unger** — anti-necessitarian institutional experimentalism; **Sabel and Zeitlin** — experimentalist governance as recursive revision.
- **Arthur** — increasing returns and lock-in; the feedback that makes canalization endogenous.
- **Zittrain** — generativity; the criterion for the open substrate.
- **Strange** — structural power; the paper's landscape power as its dynamic, topological specification. **Pistor** — law as the code that makes capital durable; law as landscape technology.
- **Polanyi** — markets as institutionally constituted. **Mazzucato** — public investment upstream of private innovation.
- **Hayek** — dispersed knowledge, turned: plural discovery procedures against the identification of discovery with one organizational form.
- **Gibson** — affordances; what an environment offers an agent, the psychological ancestor of reachability.
- **Kauffman** — the adjacent possible; what monopoly destroys.
- **Deleuze** — control as modulation; landscape operations in the register of the control society.
- **Cerf and Kahn**; **Leiner et al.**; **CERN's 1993 public-domain declaration** — the contrastive genealogy's primary record.
- **The Digital Markets Act** — the interoperability obligation as an existing, small version of fork-keeping.

Not to be cited: any paper of this institute's.
